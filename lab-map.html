Urine strip guide

These screening results are interpreted alongside symptoms and other clinical findings. A clinician can explain what a result means for you.

Glucose

Detects sugar in urine. Glucose may be present with elevated blood glucose, including diabetes, and should be assessed with appropriate follow-up.

Typical result: Negative

Protein

Checks for protein, which is usually absent or present only in small amounts. Persistent elevation can be associated with kidney conditions.

Typical result: Negative or trace

Blood

Screens for blood in urine. Possible causes include infection, stones, kidney conditions, or injury; further assessment may be needed.

Typical result: Negative

Ketones

Detects substances produced when the body uses fat for energy. Ketones can occur with fasting, vomiting, or poorly controlled diabetes.

Typical result: Negative

pH

Measures urine acidity or alkalinity. Diet, hydration, and some health conditions can affect the result.

Typical range: About 4.5–8.0
